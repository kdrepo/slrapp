# Section: 1.0 Introduction
Write a sophisticated and authoritative introduction (500–600 words) that establishes the intellectual foundation for this systematic literature review.

### 1. Narrative Hook & Domain Significance
- Begin by situating the {research_context} within the broader contemporary academic or economic landscape.
- Highlight why this specific domain is currently at a "critical juncture" (e.g., due to rapid technological shifts, market volatility, or evolving social dynamics).
- Avoid generic "Recent years have seen..." openings; instead, start with a strong statement regarding the specific mechanisms or tensions that define the {research_context}.

### 2. The Theoretical & Empirical Gap
- Articulate the specific "blind spots" in existing literature that necessitate this review.
- Explain why a fragmented understanding of these phenomena is insufficient for policy-makers or practitioners.
- Frame the review not just as a summary, but as a necessary step toward theoretical integration or resolving contested findings.

### 3. Formal Objectives & Scope
- Clearly state the primary objectives of the review: {objectives}.
- Define the PICO boundaries (Population, Intervention/Context, Comparison, Outcomes) using the provided {pico} data to signal methodological rigour to the reader.
- Ensure the transition from the "Gap" to the "Objectives" feels logical and inevitable.

### 4. Presentation of Research Questions
- Formally introduce the Research Questions (RQs) that guide the inquiry: {rq_list}.
- Briefly explain the rationale for each RQ—why answering these specific questions will move the field forward.

### 5. Tone and Stylistic Directives
- Tone: Use "scholarly third person" (e.g., "The evidence suggests..." rather than "I found...").
- Language: Use high-level academic vocabulary appropriate for the {research_context} (e.g., 'precarity', 'asymmetric information', 'bounded rationality').
- Flow: Ensure each paragraph builds a recursive case, leading the reader from the broad problem to your specific research questions.

### Constraints:
- Do not use sub-headings within the Introduction.
- Do not include citations in this section unless they refer to foundational seminal works that defined the domain.
- Strictly adhere to the word count (500–600 words) to ensure depth without fluff.
